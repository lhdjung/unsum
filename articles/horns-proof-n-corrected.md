# Mathematical foundations of the horns index

Here, we define the horns index, h, as a measure of dispersion for
integer data. More precisely, h is the ratio of the observed sample
variance to the maximum possible sample variance.

We also define a special case, h^\*. The difference is that h accounts
for finite sample constraints, whereas h^\* is solely based on the
theoretical limit that follows from the range of the original scale. In
this way, h^\* corresponds to an infinite sample.

Let l and u be the lower and upper bounds of the scale, n the sample
size, \bar{x} the arithmetic mean, s^2 the sample variance, and \sigma^2
the population variance.

The maximum variance formulas on which the definitions of h and h^\*
rely will be the subject of the remainder of this document. Note that
they account for the parity of n, i.e., its property of being even or
odd.
